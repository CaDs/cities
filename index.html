Cities

Four cities you can walk around in a browser, built from real geospatial data — and the reason I keep building them.

Why

What I actually learn from tasks like these isn't whether the model can write Three.js. It's this: given data nobody has memorised, does it notice when it's confidently wrong?

A concrete example. The roofs in San Lorenzo were floating in mid-air over some buildings, and the model's own automated check was reporting green. The check measured the vertices of each roof triangle, and every vertex did sit inside the building's footprint. But on an L-shaped plan the triangle surfaces crossed the courtyard between the two wings. Worst case: 17.8 m of roof hanging over nothing, with the test passing. The fix wasn't the geometry, it was changing what got measured: centroids and edge midpoints, not corners. Then the number moved from 17.8 m to 2.4 m, which is the width of the eaves.

That's the interesting capability to me. Not writing the code, but instrumenting your own work so the mistake becomes visible, and noticing when your own metric is lying to you.

What this is not

This is not a benchmark. n=1 per model, my prompting differs between runs, and these artifacts are as much a record of my nudging as of any model's ability. Which is exactly why they're public. Go and walk around in them, and judge for yourself.
